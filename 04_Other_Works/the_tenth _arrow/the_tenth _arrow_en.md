📌 GENERAL INFORMATION
Working Title: The Tenth Arrow (十之箭)
Genre: Mythological Fantasy, Epic Drama, Parable
Format: Animated Feature Film / Fantasy Drama Mini-Series
Target Audience: 12+ (broad audience, fans of deep mythological plots and hidden metaphors)
🎬 LOGLINE
When the prideful celestial archer Hou Yi slays nine sun-sons of the Supreme God instead of subduing them, he is stripped of his immortality and exiled to Earth. To save humanity from the famine of an eternal full moon, he is forced to sacrifice his greatest love and return his wife to the skies, only to later face a tragic betrayal by the long-lost tenth arrow—which becomes not a curse, but the ultimate key to his true apotheosis and eternal reunion.
📜 CONCEPT & CORE MYTHOLOGICAL DEVIATIONS
The project offers a profound psychological and philosophical deconstruction of the classical Chinese myth, weaving natural phenomena and feng shui talismans into a tragic love story.
The Trial of Compassion (Symbolism of the 10 Arrows): Unlike the canon, where Di Jun gives arrows for a punitive mission, here the quiver of 10 arrows is a silent test. Ready to sacrifice his own sons for the sake of the Earth, the God tests Hou Yi’s heart. The archer interprets the order too literally ("10 arrows = 10 kills"), failing the test of mercy.
The New Mechanics of the Lunar Cycle: Chang'e is the Goddess of the Waning Moon, while the Jade Rabbit is the Deity of the Waxing Moon. The Rabbit grinds the flowers of the eternal cinnamon tree into a golden light-powder, filling the lunar disk until the full moon. Chang'e, in her true zoomorphic form (the Three-Legged Toad), devours this powder, triggering the waning cycle necessary for plant growth on Earth.
The True Nature of the Money Toad: Being a mortal on Earth, Hou Yi cannot see the true divine form of his visiting wife. Chang'e descends to him as a three-legged toad, carrying lunar light in her mouth. Upon touching the ground, this celestial light turns into gold coins. The Money Toad from feng shui is Chang'e, weeping tears of gold from longing for her husband.
The Tenth Arrow as a Ritual of Return: The treacherous shot by his disciple Feng Meng from an ambush is fired using the very same lost tenth arrow of Di Jun. This turns the murder into a cosmic act of justice. The Heavens accept Hou Yi's life as payment for the last saved sun, equating his sacrifice to that of a god.
📖 EXTENDED SYNOPSIS
Act I: Pride and Exile
A Great Drought plagues the Earth: ten solar ravens, the sons of the Supreme God Di Jun, have lit the sky simultaneously. The mortal ruler Yao begs the Heavens for mercy. Di Jun sends the greatest and proudest celestial, the archer Hou Yi, giving him a crimson bow and a quiver of 10 white arrows. Di Jun gives no direct orders to kill; it is a test: can the great warrior show compassion to his foolish sun-brothers and weak mortals?
Blinded by his own power, Hou Yi decides that the number of arrows equals the number of targets. He methodically shoots down nine suns. Realizing that the death of the last sun will destroy the world, King Yao secretly sends a servant to steal the tenth arrow from the quiver. One sun is left alive. The people praise the hero, but the grieving Di Jun is enraged by the archer's cruelty. He strips Hou Yi of his immortality and exiles him to Earth as a mortal. His loyal and loving wife, Chang'e, voluntarily shares his fate and falls to Earth with him.
Act II: Mortal Love and the Great Sacrifice
Suffering on Earth, Hou Yi is filled with resentment toward the gods. However, human kindness, care, and friendship gradually thaw his heart. Experiencing mortal hunger, cold, and poverty for the first time, he begins to understand Di Jun's parental pain and genuinely connects with humanity. Suddenly, he realizes a terrifying truth: Chang'e is the goddess of the waning moon, who ensures the lunar cycle. While she is mortal and trapped on Earth, the Moon will freeze in a phase of eternal full moon, plants will stop bearing seeds, and humanity will perish from a new famine.
Driven by love for the people and his wife, Hou Yi performs a series of heroic deeds, destroying earthly monsters to catch the attention of the gods. He reaches Xi Wangmu (the Queen Mother of the West) and receives the elixir of immortality, but with a cruel catch: if they drink it together, they will remain immortal but trapped on a dying Earth forever. If only one drinks it, that person will return to the skies. To avoid breaking Di Jun's decree (as the Moon is near the Heavens, but not in the celestial courts themselves) and to save the planet's ecosystem, Hou Yi makes a heavy choice. On the night that became the Mid-Autumn Festival (Zhongqiujie), he forces Chang'e to drink the elixir. She flies to the sky, restoring the lunar cycle for the sake of all life.
Act III: The Closed Circle and True Apotheosis
The lunar cycle is restored, crops grow, and the world is saved. Hou Yi is a true hero of the Earth, but his character deteriorates rapidly from loneliness and unbearable longing. Chang'e also suffers in her Lunar Palace. During the new moon, she secretly descends to her husband. But since Hou Yi is stripped of his divine vision, he only sees her as a silent Three-Legged Toad. She brings him lunar light in her mouth, which turns into gold on the ground. Hou Yi becomes fabulously rich, but money cannot replace his family warmth. Yet, he never once regrets his sacrifice for the people. This becomes his final, successfully passed trial.
The hero's bitter nature provokes a conspiracy among his followers. His envious disciple, Feng Meng, ambushes Hou Yi on a forest road. Feng Meng draws his bow. In his hands is the very same lost tenth arrow of Di Jun, found after many years. The arrow pierces Hou Yi's back. But this is not a mere murder: the celestial arrow ends the hero's earthly suffering, accepting his life as an equivalent to the sun's. The Heavens recognize his grand sacrifice.
Hou Yi's soul returns in triumph to the pantheon as the powerful deity Zongbu—the ruler of the underworld and the ghosts of China. Having regained his divine vision, Hou Yi no longer sees his wife as a toad. Now, every night of the new moon, while the Jade Rabbit is just beginning to grind his powder, two equal and great deities reunite in their eternal love, and on Earth, people honor Chang'e as the Money Toad, bringing the frozen lunar light into their homes.
The end
